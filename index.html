Guiding Question:

When a security incident happens, how should an organization respond in order—and how does it decide which risks to address first?

1. Security Incidents & Textbook Table (Textbook p. 49)

Information leak, data tampering, and service outage

Even a well-protected system can be attacked. When a security incident occurs—such as data leaked, files tampered with, or a service knocked offline—a rushed and unplanned reaction only makes the damage worse. Organizations follow a clear, ordered response so that damage is contained, the cause is removed, and the same problem does not return.

Type Description
Information leak Personal information or confidential information is leaked to the outside.
Data tampering Data within a system is altered without authorization.
Service outage A service becomes unavailable due to attacks on the server, etc.

2. The 6 Stages of Incident Response (Textbook p. 49-50)

Why order matters: Preparation, Detection, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, Prevention

When a security incident occurs, response proceeds through six ordered stages. It is vital to proceed in order. For example, if recovery is performed without identifying the root cause (eradication), the same incident may occur again.

3. Risk Assessment & Evaluation Matrix (Textbook p. 50-51)

Risk Size = Impact × Likelihood

Because no team can fix everything at once, organizations prioritize risks based on two factors:

Impact: How much damage occurs if the risk actually materializes.
Likelihood: How high the probability is that the risk will actually materialize.
Formula: Risk Size = Impact × Likelihood. High-impact and high-likelihood risks must be addressed first!

Think It Through & Case Study Breakouts

Containment vs Eradication (Textbook p. 50)

Why must containment come before eradication in the six-stage response?

Curriculum Answer: Containment limits the scope of impact and stops the damage from spreading further across the network. If you attempt eradication or recovery first without containing the threat, active malware could reinfect systems or cause further leaks while you work.

Welcome students to Be School! Here is your quick revision of Lesson 2-3: Incident Response and Risk Management. When a security incident like an information leak, data tampering, or service outage occurs, organizations follow six ordered stages: preparation, detection, containment, eradication, recovery, and prevention of recurrence. Order is vital because recovering without eradicating the root cause lets the incident return. Furthermore, organizations prioritize threats using risk assessment, calculating risk size as impact multiplied by likelihood, and addressing high impact and high likelihood risks first.

Prompt: Why must containment come before eradication in the six-stage response?

Prompt: Explain why the six stages of incident response must be carried out in order, using recovery and eradication as an example. [6 Marks] In your answer: refer to what happens if the cause is not removed.
Pillar 1: Fixed Ordered Sequence of Stages [2 / 2 Marks]

Explains that incident response follows a strict logical sequence (Preparation → Detection → Containment → Eradication → Recovery → Prevention).

Pillar 2: Flaw of Recovering Without Eradication [2 / 2 Marks]

Explains that if recovery is performed before eradicating the root cause (malware/vulnerability), the restored system remains vulnerable and the same incident will recur.

Pillar 3: Conclusion on System Stability [2 / 2 Marks]

Synthesizes how following the ordered stages ensures long-term security and permanent elimination of threats.
